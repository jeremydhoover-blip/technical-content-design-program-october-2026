# Before Week 3

---

## Read

Review your `content/foundations/grammar-and-style.md`. Read it as if you were an AI assistant being asked to use it as a writing guide.

Think about: what would an AI assistant need to know about your product to write copy in your voice? What's missing from your files? What's too vague to act on?

Finish `grammar-and-style.md` as assigned after Week 2. Replace its placeholders with your writing conventions, terminology decisions, and examples. Keep the template headings.

---

## Do

If you haven't yet, finish your `personas.md` file. You need at least two personas, both sections filled in. Partial personas won't give you useful results in Week 3's demo.

Come to the session with:
- `voice-and-tone.md`: all sections complete, including tone by context and anti-patterns
- `personas.md`: both personas filled in
- `grammar-and-style.md`: all sections complete, including terminology and examples

Run `npm run check` and read the warnings. These three files should have no unfinished placeholders. You will complete component patterns and accessibility after Week 3.

---

## What to have ready

- Your fork is up to date and running locally
- All changes from Week 2 are committed, pushed, and merged
- You've run `npm run dev` recently and confirmed the app still works
- **Your coding assistant from Week 1 still works** in VS Code, Codex, Claude, or another coding environment. Ask it to read and explain one foundations file, then confirm it read the right file. See [Set up your coding assistant](../../docs/SETUP.md#set-up-your-coding-assistant-week-1).
- `npm run doctor` shows no `[fail]` lines
- You can point your assistant to a specific repo file. You do not need to connect an AI service to the learning app.
