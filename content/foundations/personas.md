# Personas: The Saizen

> **Guide:** 

These are the primary users of The Saizen. Use them to check whether a string would land: would this person understand it, and would it frustrate them?

## Persona 1: Jason, someone who missed out on what happened in music the past 15 years

**Role:** Engineer
**Experience level:** Familiar with J-Rock bands that have gone viral
**How they learn:** YouTube videos and Discords 

### Who they are

> **Guide:** 3 or 4 sentences. What is their day like and where does your product fit in it? What do they care about more than your product?

Jason reads The Saizen during his daily train commute to learn about other bands similar to the few J-rock bands he knows and likes. He doesn’t have a lot of time to explore all the bands from the world’s second largest music scene so he’s trying to narrow down his options. He looks for bands inspired by Western bands he grew up loving. 

### Goals

- Discover a new band he never heard of before
- Explore their discography to choose an album to listen to
- Trust that the writer has a similar vibe to his own

### Pain points

- Explanations in regards to music theory that are inaccurate
- Too many equally weighted albums in terms of reviews
- Descriptions of songs that don’t take into account what the artists meant

### Vocabulary they use

> **Guide:** The real words this person says. This is the most useful section for an AI. If they say "stuff", do not write "assets".

Chromatic, Double bass, Half-time, Chaotic

### What they need from copy

- Musical explanations - how are the instruments complimenting each other
- Sources of inspiration - what are the favorite artists of the artists
- Cultural references - Explanations of what Japanese words mean and why they were used

## Persona 2: Michelle, A 17-year-old girl who's interested in playing rock music with higher technicallity

**Role:** High school junior
**Experience level:** Lives on TikTok and never uses a desktop.
**How they learn:** Watches music videos and guitar tutorial videos

### Who they are

Michele, in part, needs inspiration to achieve her dream of being a guitar goddess. She needs to know that there are women who play at very high levels and succeed despite playing in a male-dominated industry. 
### Goals

- Explore numerous female guiarists with similar playing styles to her so she can start to figure out her own.
- Find videos that show them performing play-throughs and learn those songs.
- Find a community of other like-minded young female musicians for conversations and to maybe even form a band

### Pain points

- She's not well-versed in music theory so technical aspects may allude her early on
- Trying to find content that reflect her interest. 
- She doesn't read or speak Japanese so it's difficult to do more than just guitar covers of j-rock songs.

### Vocabulary they use

Shredding, Vibe, Goated, Tapping

### What they need from copy

- Techncal explanations of composition, music theory and playing style with definitions written at an 8th grade reading level
- The back stories of the guitarists who inspire her: how did they acheive their goals; what obstacles did they overcome; what new challenges do they face?
- Reminders that bands are collaborative efforts, and in j-rock, bass and drums are lead instruments as importat as guitar

## How to use these personas

> **Guide:** Write 3 short rules for how a writer or an AI should apply these personas. Keep the rule about conflicts: what to do when both personas need different things from the same string.

- **When writing:** Ask yourself what level of technical writing is acceptable for both personals and write for the commonalities.
- **When reviewing:** Use the personals to understand which vocabulary would not turn off either persona. Use slang and jargon understandable to different generations.
- **When the personas conflict:** Choose content that is least likely to make either personal not come back. Copy that's too masculine fails Michelle. Copy that's too Generation Z or Alpha Generation fails Jason.
