## The voice

The voice is who The Saizen is, in every context. It stays consistent.

> **Guide:** Write 3 to 5 attributes. Use the pattern **Quality, not its extreme.** Then one or two sentences saying what that means in practice and what to avoid. Make each attribute testable: could someone check a string against it?

**Vivid: The writing must mirror the high-octane energy of anime openings.
• Do: Describe the physical aesthetic, costume shifts, and theatrical vibe of a performance to transport the reader to the venue.

**Hyper-Technical: The copy needs to reflect the intricateness and technicality of j-rock’s compositions.
• Do: Name-drop specific gear, time signatures, scale modulations, or intricate slap-bass and guitar techniques that highlight the musician's virtuosity.

**Oshi: The tone should embrace a high-energy, intense bond between artists and fandom.
• Do: Match the raw, emotional enthusiasm of the fandom by celebrating a band member's specific stage persona and creative growth.

**Subculture-Savvy: The site must read like an ultimate insider who fluently navigates distinct Japanese live-music subcultures.
• Do: Naturally integrate accurate scene vocabulary like live house, bandoman, or subgenre-specific terms when contextualizing a band's history.

### What the voice is not

> **Guide:** Name 3 or 4 things your copy must never sound like. Add a short example of each in quotation marks.

- Not vague: "They play really fast"
- Not cynical: "This is a band for weebs."
- Not generic Western cliche: "Avoid jargon (like "playing the club circuit") that erases the distinct cultural setting of the Japanese music scene."

## The tone by context

The voice stays the same. The tone shifts with what the user is doing and how they feel.

> **Guide:** For each context, write 2 sentences describing the user's state and the tone to use. Then add one good example and one not-example. If your product has contexts that matter more (payments, safety, offline), add a section for them.

### Onboarding

The user is typically a Western newcomer who is deeply fascinated by J-Rock's unique energy but lacks cultural context and familiarity with the language. To meet them where they are, the tone must be enthusiastically welcoming and highly educational, demystifying complex scene dynamics without ever sounding condescending or exclusionary.

**Good:**
> "You’ve just unlocked a front-row pass to the wildest music scene on earth."

**Not:**
> "Thank you for creating an account on our music database web application."

### Empty states

When the user is served an empty page or no search results, use the vernacular of their interests to guide them onward.
**Good:**
> "This stage is empty, let's find another."
> "No such artist exists" Check the spelling or try another name."

**Not:**
> "This page is under construction"
> "Oopsies, try again."

### Errors

Follow these guidelines for error state messaging. 

**Good:**
> "Sorry, an amp blew. Refresh the page to get it cranking again."

**Not:**
> "We're experiencing an error. Please refresh the page."

### Confirmations

The user placed an order. Let them know simply, but not in a boring way.

**Good:**
> "Your music is on its way."

**Not:**
> "Your order is being processed and will be shipped soon."

## Anti-patterns

> **Guide:** Every anti-pattern here can become an eval in Week 4, so make each one specific enough to check. Use the format: **Pattern**: bad example, then good example.

Avoid these in all copy for The Saizen:

- **Exclusionary Gatekeeper**: "If you want to support your oshi, you need to buy tickets to the taiban at a local live house instead of waiting for a major debut." becomes "If you want to support your absolute favorite band member (your oshi), the best way is to catch them live at a taiban, a high-energy showcase where multiple underground bands share the stage at a local indie venue."
- **Anime-Only Caricature**: "Get ready to rock out with awesome anime vibes, subbed and dubbed! Kawaii!!!" becomes "Beyond the iconic 90-second anime opening, this track reveals its true depth through a sprawling, cinematic arrangement and an intricately layered string section."
- **Western-Centric Lazy Compariso**: "Think of this group as the Japanese Green Day" becomes "Emerging from the gritty underground scene of the late 90s, this quartet pioneered a distinct brand of melodic punk characterized by lightning-fast tempos and deeply emotional, introspective Japanese lyricism."
