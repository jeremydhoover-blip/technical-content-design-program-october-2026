# Personas: Luma

> **Worked example.** This is a finished file for Luma, a fictional team workflow app. Read it to see what done looks like. Write your own for your product instead of copying this one.

This file describes the primary users of Luma. Reference these personas when you're writing new copy, evaluating whether a string will land with your audience, or deciding how much context to include in a message.

Personas aren't demographic profiles. They describe goals, pain points, vocabulary, and what users need from the interface. Use them to pressure-test your copy: would this person understand this string? Would it frustrate them? Would they even notice it?

---

## Persona 1: Maya, Mid-level individual contributor

**Role:** Operations analyst / project coordinator  
**Experience level:** Comfortable with digital tools, not a technical user  
**Product familiarity:** Learns by doing; reads documentation when stuck

### Who she is

Maya manages day-to-day workflows for a team of 8. She uses Luma multiple times a day, mostly to track status and coordinate handoffs. She's not interested in the product itself. She's interested in getting her work done. She'll figure out what she needs to know, but she doesn't have time to learn features she doesn't need yet.

### Goals

- Complete tasks quickly without reading long instructions
- Know at a glance what needs her attention
- Understand what a change will do before she makes it
- Share work with teammates without extra steps
- Find things she worked on before without digging

### Pain points

- Interface copy that assumes she already knows how the system works
- Buttons or actions that don't explain what they do
- Error messages that don't tell her how to fix the problem
- Confirmation dialogs that are unclear about what will actually happen

### Vocabulary she uses

"my stuff," "tasks," "updates," "check in," "share," "notes," "due date," "assigned to me"

### What she needs from copy

- Short labels that get to the point
- Contextual help that appears when she needs it, not before
- Clear confirmation before any irreversible action
- Error messages that tell her what to do next

### What frustrates her in a product

- Having to click through multiple screens to do a simple thing
- Microcopy that's cute but not useful ("Looks like you don't have anything here yet!")
- Inconsistent terminology (sometimes "task," sometimes "item," sometimes "card")
- Loading states with no indication of progress

## Persona 2: Tariq, Senior technical user

**Role:** Product engineer / technical lead  
**Experience level:** High; uses multiple technical tools daily  
**Product familiarity:** Reads release notes; explores settings; files bug reports

### Who he is

Tariq uses Luma alongside a stack of developer tools. He's efficient and opinionated. He trusts systems that behave predictably and has a low tolerance for copy that's vague or inconsistent. He often extends or integrates tools, so he notices things like naming inconsistencies between the UI and the API.

### Goals

- Move through the interface efficiently without friction
- Understand system behavior from interface copy alone
- Trust that confirmation dialogs and error messages are accurate
- Find advanced settings and documentation without escalating to support
- Know exactly what an action will do before triggering it

### Pain points

- Error messages that are technically inaccurate or too vague to act on
- UI copy that doesn't match API terminology
- Confirmation dialogs that are overly cautious about routine actions
- Onboarding flows that slow him down when he already knows what he's doing

### Vocabulary he uses

"config," "trigger," "deploy," "endpoint," "token," "run," "log," "status," "integrate"

### What he needs from copy

- Technical accuracy. If something fails, say exactly why
- Consistent naming between UI, docs, and API
- Progressive disclosure. Don't front-load context he doesn't need
- Trust that the interface won't do unexpected things

### What frustrates him in a product

- Overly cheerful empty states that get in the way of actual work
- Sloppy use of technical terms ("update" vs. "sync" vs. "refresh")
- Confirmation dialogs for trivially reversible actions
- Copy that talks down to him or explains the obvious

## How to use these personas

**When writing copy:** Ask yourself which persona is most likely to encounter this string in context. Write for them first, then check whether it still works for the other.

**When reviewing copy:** Use the personas to gut-check vocabulary, tone, and level of detail. Would Maya understand this without reading the docs? Would Tariq find this condescending?

**When evaluating AI-generated content:** These personas are part of your evaluation criteria. Copy that's too casual fails Tariq. Copy that's too dense fails Maya. Aim for the overlap.
